# Meal Planner MVP Product Specification

## Product vision

Meal Planner is a chat-first recipe book and weekly planning assistant. It remembers a household's schedule, dietary requirements, preferences, and recent meals; proposes a practical dinner plan; and turns the approved plan into exact recipes and a shopping list.

The source code will be open source. Users may self-host it and supply their own model endpoint, while a future managed service can include hosting and model inference as a paid, batteries-included option.

## MVP interfaces

- Telegram bot
- Simple hosted web chat

WhatsApp support is deferred because its approval, integration, and messaging-cost constraints make it less suitable for the first release.

## Users and households

- A household may contain one or more users.
- Every household member can edit its plans, recipes, preferences, and shopping lists.
- Data is isolated by household.
- Preferences can belong to the household or an individual member.
- Hard constraints apply to everyone eating a meal.
- Soft preferences can conflict and should be balanced over time.
- Attendance matters: when a member is away, meals may favor the preferences of those present.
- Telegram identities will be linked to household membership. The web interface will initially use magic-link authentication.

## Weekly planning workflow

1. At a configurable day and time, the application creates a draft plan for the following week. The initial default is Saturday morning in the household's timezone.
2. The draft contains dinner titles and short descriptions, but not newly generated full recipes.
3. Household members review and modify the draft through chat. They can state nights out, request replacements, change portions, or override preferences.
4. A household member confirms the plan.
5. Confirmation triggers creation or selection of exact recipes and generation of the shopping list.
6. If nobody confirms the draft, no full recipes or shopping list are generated for that week.

The MVP plans dinners only. It plans seven days by default, with the planning period intended to become configurable later. Portions default to the number of household members attending each dinner.

Leftovers may naturally become lunch the next day, but explicit leftover scheduling and lunch planning are deferred.

## Planning inputs and priorities

The planner considers:

- Allergies and dietary restrictions as hard constraints
- Nights when household members are away
- Household and individual dislikes
- Preferred cuisines and desired variety
- Cooking-time and budget preferences
- Recently cooked meals, initially using an eight-week history
- Ingredients used in the previous week that may still be available
- Opportunities to reuse fresh ingredients across the proposed week

The application will not maintain exact pantry inventory. Previous-week ingredient reuse should therefore be conservative and shelf-life-aware rather than assuming every unused ingredient remains available.

## Recipe book

The recipe book stores complete recipes, including ingredients, quantities, portions, and instructions.

Recipes may be:

- Submitted manually by a household member
- Imported from a permitted website
- Generated by an LLM

Plans should reuse suitable stored recipes where possible. New recipes are generated only when requested or when no suitable stored recipe exists. This reduces inference cost and makes the household recipe book increasingly valuable.

Imported recipes retain their source URL and author attribution. Import should prefer machine-readable recipe metadata such as Schema.org `Recipe` data and must respect website terms and applicable content rights. A future shared recipe catalogue is outside the MVP.

## Shopping lists

After plan confirmation, the application combines recipe ingredients into a household shopping list.

- Compatible units and duplicate ingredients should be normalized where practical.
- Configurable household staples are excluded; the initial list can be seeded with basics such as oil, salt, and common spices.
- The application does not track pantry quantities.
- Household members can add one-off items by chat.
- Manually added items remain until checked off or the weekly list is archived.
- Supermarket-specific aisle ordering is outside the MVP.

## Conversational behavior

Chat is the main control surface. Users should be able to express requests naturally, including:

- "I'm out on Tuesday."
- "Replace Friday with something vegetarian."
- "Alex is away, so make something spicy."
- "We are out of dishwashing liquid."
- "Save this recipe."
- "Don't suggest that meal again."

The application should translate these messages into explicit, reviewable changes to structured household data. Deterministic application logic—not the model alone—must enforce permissions, hard dietary constraints, confirmation state, and list updates.

## Model providers

Self-hosted users can bring their own model credentials. The MVP provider configuration includes:

- OpenAI-compatible base URL
- Model name
- API key stored as a secret
- Presets for OpenAI and OpenRouter
- A separate Anthropic adapter for Claude's native API

OpenAI-compatible self-hosted models are supported, although model capability may affect planning quality. The design should keep core state and validation outside provider-specific prompts.

The managed service may later offer included inference rather than requiring users to supply credentials.

## Deployment and privacy

- The project is open source and designed for self-hosting.
- A managed cloud edition may become a paid product.
- Hosted deployments use the chosen cloud provider's secret storage for model credentials.
- Self-hosted deployments control their own storage and secret-management environment.
- Dietary data, chat history, recipes, schedules, and credentials are private household data.
- Account deletion and data export should be supported before a public managed launch.

## Deferred features

- WhatsApp integration
- Breakfast, lunch, and snack planning
- Explicit leftover scheduling
- Full pantry quantities and expiry tracking
- Supermarket aisle or store-specific integrations
- Shared public recipe catalogue
- Managed subscriptions and bundled inference
- Planning periods other than the next seven days

## MVP success criteria

A household can:

1. Join through Telegram or the web interface.
2. Record its members, dietary constraints, preferences, regular nights out, timezone, and planning schedule.
3. Add or import complete recipes.
4. Receive a relevant seven-day dinner draft automatically.
5. Revise and confirm that draft through chat.
6. Receive exact recipes with appropriate portions and a consolidated shopping list.
7. Add and check off one-off shopping items through chat.
8. Use either configured BYO model credentials or an OpenAI-compatible self-hosted endpoint.
